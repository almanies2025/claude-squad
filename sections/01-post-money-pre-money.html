Real Talk: Pre-money valuation is what the company is worth before the investor writes a check. Post-money is what it's worth after — the pre-money plus the fresh capital. The VC Method works backwards from an ambitious exit valuation to tell you what the company is worth today. The trap students fall into: mixing up which number goes where, or forgetting that the option pool lives inside the pre-money (which dilutes founders more than VCs expect).
POST-MONEY = PRE-MONEY + Investment  |  PRE-MONEY = POST-MONEY − Investment  |  VC Method: Pre-money = Exit Value / (1+Target IRR)n  |  Ownership% = Investment / Post-money
WORKED EXAMPLE — VC Method (FNCE696 Style)
Given: Angel invests $500K for 20% equity  |  Target exit: $20M in Year 5  |  Target IRR: 40%  |  Option pool: 10% post-money
STEP 1 — Find Post-money from ownership stake
Post-money = Investment / Ownership%
Post-money = $500,000 / 20% = $2,500,000
Intuition: paying 20% for $500K means the whole company is worth $2.5M after the check clears.

STEP 2 — Back out Pre-money (before the $500K)
Pre-money = Post-money − Investment
Pre-money = $2,500,000 − $500,000 = $2,000,000
Intuition: $500K is new money — the old ownership pie (founders) was worth $2M before it arrived.

STEP 3 — Verify with VC Method (present value of exit)
PV = Exit Value / (1+IRR)n
PV = $20,000,000 / (1.40)5
(1.40)5 = 5.3783
PV = $20,000,000 / 5.3783 = $3,719,000 ≈ $3.72M
Intuition: at 40% annual returns, $3.72M today grows to $20M in 5 years. This is what the 20% stake costs in present-value terms.

STEP 4 — Check: does the angel's $500K buy 20% of $3.72M?
$500K / $3.72M = 13.4% — not 20%!
The angel's $500K buys 20% of the $2.5M post-money (Step 1), which corresponds to a deemed pre-money of $2M.
The VC Method here produces a higher valuation ($3.72M) because the 40% IRR target exceeds the implied 4x return from the $500K / $2.5M deal.

ANSWERS:
Post-money valuation = $2,500,000
Pre-money valuation = $2,000,000
VC Method implied pre-money (at 40% IRR, 5-year exit) = $3,719,000
Angel ownership fraction = 20.0%
Exam Trick: When you see "option pool" mentioned, ask — is it pre-money or post-money? YC and most VCs require the option pool inside pre-money, which means founders absorb that dilution, not the incoming investor.
