Real Talk: Convertible notes are actual debt — they accrue interest and have a maturity date when they must be repaid (or converted). Unlike SAFEs, the interest earned compounds the principal, so the investor effectively converts more shares at the next round. The cap and discount stack: the note holder gets whichever produces more shares — the lower of (cap-hit price) vs (discounted round price). The classic founder mistake: ignoring the interest accrual, which silently inflates the conversion amount.
Accrued interest = Principal × Rate × Years  |  Total conversion amount = Principal + Accrued interest  |  Conversion price = MIN(Cap / Pre-money shares, Round price × (1−d))  |  Shares received = Total conversion amount / Conversion price
WORKED EXAMPLE — Convertible Note: Interest + Cap + Discount
Given: Principal = $500,000  |  Coupon rate = 8% per annum  |  Holding period = 2 years  |  Valuation cap = $4,000,000  |  Discount = 20%  |  Series B pre-money = $15,000,000  |  Pre-money shares outstanding = 2,000,000 (Seed + SAFEs + Founders)
STEP 1 — Compute accrued interest (compound, simple annual)
Accrued interest = Principal × Rate × Years
Accrued interest = $500,000 × 8% × 2 = $80,000
Intuition: interest is the cost of delaying equity conversion. The VC round is 2 years away, so the investor is "owed" $80K on top of principal.

STEP 2 — Compute total conversion amount (principal + interest)
Total = $500,000 + $80,000 = $580,000
Intuition: the note investor converts $580K, not $500K — the interest buys extra shares.

STEP 3 — Compute round price per share (Series B)
Round price = Pre-money / Pre-money shares
Round price = $15,000,000 / 2,000,000 = $7.50/share

STEP 4 — Compute the two conversion prices
Path A — Discount method:
Discount price = Round price × (1−Discount)
Discount price = $7.50 × 0.80 = $6.00/share

Path B — Cap method:
Cap price = Cap / Pre-money shares
Cap price = $4,000,000 / 2,000,000 = $2.00/share
Intuition: the cap of $4M on 2M shares means $2.00/share equivalent — the investor gets in at a 73% discount to the $7.50 Series B price!

STEP 5 — Pick the LOWER price (more shares for the note holder)
MIN($6.00, $2.00) = $2.00/share — Cap wins decisively
Intuition: when a startup does very well (Series B at $15M pre), the cap saves the note investor from paying full price. They convert at $2 instead of $6.

STEP 6 — Compute shares received on conversion
Shares = Total conversion amount / Conversion price
Shares = $580,000 / $2.00 = 290,000 shares

STEP 7 — Check effective price paid vs Series B investors
Series B investors pay $7.50/share. Note investor effectively pays:
$580,000 / 290,000 = $2.00/share (because cap wins)
Savings: $5.50/share vs Series B investors, or $1,595,000 in foregone investment on 290,000 shares.

ANSWERS:
Accrued interest = $80,000
Total conversion amount = $580,000
Conversion price = $2.00/share (cap wins over 20% discount)
Note holder shares received = 290,000 shares
Note holder effective discount vs Series B = 73.3%
Compare to SAFE: A $500K SAFE with same $4M cap and 20% discount in the same Series B round — no interest accrual, so SAFE converts $500K (not $580K). The note holder gets 290K shares vs SAFE holder's 250K shares. The 2 years of interest bought 40,000 extra shares.
Exam Trap: If you forget to add accrued interest to principal before computing shares, you'll undercount the note holder's shares. Always compound-first, then convert. Also note: interest is typically simple annual (not compound) unless stated otherwise in the note terms.
