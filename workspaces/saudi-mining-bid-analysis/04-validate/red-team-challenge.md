## Assumptions That May Be Wrong

### 1. Chinese Policy Financing Is Assumed But Not Confirmed

The analysis presents Chinese policy bank financing (EXIM Bank, CDB at 2–4%) as a baseline differentiator, leading to a 15–22% IRR estimate. However:

- Saudi Arabia is not a traditional Belt and Road Initiative recipient — Chinese policy banks have limited track record there compared to Pakistan, Africa, or Southeast Asia
- No indicative term sheet has been confirmed
- If policy financing is unavailable, the company is financed at Saudi commercial rates (6–8%), and the realistic IRR drops to 8–14%

**Challenge:** Does the 15–22% range assume policy financing that must be confirmed, or has a financing commitment already been secured?

**Consequence if wrong:** IRR overestimated by 3–5 percentage points. Every capital allocation decision tied to this contract would be wrong.

### 2. The Market Remains Open for Future Contracts

Saudi construction contract awards fell 60% in 2025 (from $71B to under $30B). More competition for fewer contracts changes the return on investment for JV formation and market entry spending.

**Challenge:** Is the market entry investment justified given the 2025 construction market contraction?

**Consequence if wrong:** Market entry costs exceed opportunity, particularly if the existing EPC contract is the only near-term revenue source.

### 3. The MIMR Contact Problem Is a Future Risk, Not a Present One

If prior interactions with the MIMR contact involved any exchange of tender-specific information, evaluation data, or procurement advantage, criminal exposure already exists — before any further money is spent on market entry.

**Challenge:** Has any legal review of past MIMR contact interactions been conducted?

**Consequence if wrong:** Criminal exposure already exists. Spending on market entry does not reduce it.

### 4. JV Partner Search Is Achievable in 4–8 Weeks

Finding a credible Saudi partner with financial strength, relevant experience, Nitaqat capacity, and aligned incentives takes 6–18 months under normal conditions — not weeks.

**Challenge:** Is 4–8 weeks realistic for identifying candidates and beginning discussions?

**Consequence if wrong:** Future contract pipeline inaccessible because pre-qualification cannot be met without a JV partner.

### 5. MOMRA and Etimad Registration Complete in Weeks

Saudi government administrative timelines routinely take 3–6 months for foreign companies. Etimad registration alone has documented processing times of 4–12 weeks for foreign suppliers.

**Challenge:** Have realistic registration timelines been built into the action plan?

**Consequence if wrong:** Schedule delays prevent the company from bidding on early future contracts.

## Gaps in the Analysis

### 1. No Currency Risk Quantification

SAR-denominated contract revenue vs. RMB-denominated costs and Chinese debt servicing. A 10% RMB appreciation against USD could reduce IRR by 2–4 percentage points. No sensitivity table was produced.

### 2. No Schedule Risk Quantification

Delay LD exposure (0.5–1%/week, capped 5–10%) not modeled against probability of delay. A 6-month delay on a $500M contract = $13M in LDs. Has this been modeled?

### 3. No Contract Termination Scenario

What happens if the contract is terminated for cause, convenience, or government cancellation? Advance payment refund obligations? Bank guarantee drawdown exposure?

### 4. No Saudization Cost Model

Nitaqat compliance has a direct cost — Saudi nationals command higher salaries. Has this been modeled into the IRR?

### 5. No IP Risk in JV Context

Chinese technology and operational processes exposed to Saudi JV partner who may independently compete or replicate capabilities.

## Overlooked Risks

### 1. US Secondary Sanctions Risk for Chinese SOEs (2026)

Chinese SOEs in Saudi Arabia face potential US secondary sanctions exposure in the current geopolitical environment. Affects banking relationships (SWIFT, USD correspondent banking), project co-financing, and any US-person involvement.

**Not mentioned in the original analysis.**

### 2. Saudi Government Contract Cancellation Risk

Saudi government contracts can be cancelled, scope-changed, or suspended due to budget reallocation or policy shifts. No MADCOH-style protection exists for foreign contractors.

**Not mentioned in the original analysis.**

### 3. JV Partner's Own Legal Exposure

If the Saudi JV partner has compliance issues (Nitaqat violations, labor disputes, prior corruption allegations), the Chinese company inherits that exposure through the JV entity.

**Not mentioned in the original analysis.**

### 4. Work Authorization for Chinese Personnel

Visa and work permit processing times for Chinese nationals in Saudi Arabia have lengthened. Workforce deployment capacity may be constrained.

**Not mentioned in the original analysis.**

### 5. Maaden's Financial Capacity Is Cyclical

Maaden's record profits are driven by high commodity prices. If prices correct, CAPEX could be cut. The $7.55–9.55B CAPEX guidance is not a guaranteed commitment.

**Flagged but not analyzed as a cyclical risk.**

## Internal Contradictions

### 1. MIMR Contact Is Simultaneously a Liability and a Recommended Tool

Section 1.1 recommends using the contact to "arrange an introduction" to MIMR officials. Section 2.1 identifies this same activity as potentially criminal (trading in influence). The line between legitimate introduction and illegal influence-trading is not clearly drawn.

**Resolution needed:** Precise written guidance on what the contact can do without creating criminal exposure.

### 2. "Build Saudi Track Record" vs. Immediate Parallel Workload

Recommending immediate Etimad registration, MOMRA classification, JV identification, and Saudi bank account opening — all in parallel in the first 2–4 weeks — is unrealistic for a company without basic market entry infrastructure in place.

**Resolution needed:** Prioritize. Single most important action in 30 days vs. 90 days.

### 3. JV Is "Most Important" But "4–8 Weeks" Is Unrealistic

The JV partner requirement is called "the single most important structural requirement" but given only 4–8 weeks. No credible Saudi firm completes JV due diligence and forms a legal entity in that timeframe.

**Resolution needed:** 4–8 weeks is enough to identify candidates and begin discussions. 6–18 months is realistic for JV formation. Reconcile the language.

### 4. "Compliance as Competitive Advantage" vs. Saudi Reality

The analysis says Saudi clients "actively prefer contractors with clean anti-corruption records." But Saudi construction history includes documented corruption in major projects. Is clean compliance a real differentiator or an aspirational Western framing?

## Questions Management Should Be Asking

1. Has any legal review of past MIMR contact interactions been completed? This is more urgent than any market entry step.

2. What is the actual EXIM Bank or CDB financing status — confirmed, indicative, or exploratory? The IRR model depends entirely on this.

3. Has a schedule risk analysis been done on the existing EPC contract? What is the probability-weighted LD exposure?

4. What is the Saudization cost in the current contract structure, and has it been modeled into IRR?

5. If the existing EPC contract were terminated tomorrow — for cause, convenience, or cancellation — what is the maximum financial exposure?

6. Has the company assessed US secondary sanctions exposure for its specific Saudi activities?

7. Is the company structured as a Saudi entity for tax purposes, or operating as a foreign branch? This affects both liability and tax exposure.
