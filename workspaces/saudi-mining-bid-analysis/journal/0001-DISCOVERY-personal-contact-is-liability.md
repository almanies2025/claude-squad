## Discovery: The Personal Ministry Contact Is a Liability

**The single most important finding from this analysis is that the personal connection inside the Saudi Ministry of Industry and Mineral Resources (MIMR) is a net legal liability — not a competitive asset.**

### Why This Is True

Using a personal government relationship to gain procurement advantage constitutes multiple criminal offenses under both Saudi and Chinese law:

1. **Saudi law — Royal Decree on Combating Bribery (1975):** Up to 10 years imprisonment and SAR 5M fine for giving or promising any benefit to a public official to influence an official act. The contact themselves also commits a criminal offense by providing access or information.

2. **Saudi law — Trading in influence (mutasallih):** A separate criminal offense from bribery, specifically covering the exploitation of personal relationships with government officials for procurement advantage. Up to 7 years imprisonment.

3. **Chinese Criminal Law Article 391:** Bribery of foreign public officials is a criminal offense in China, with extraterritorial reach. The same act could result in prosecution in both jurisdictions simultaneously.

4. **China's Anti-Foreign Bribery Law (2021):** Applies to Chinese companies and nationals operating abroad. Maximum penalty: 10+ years imprisonment, unlimited fines. Enforcement against overseas SOEs has increased significantly since 2020.

### What This Means

The question is not "how can we use this contact ethically?" The question is "how do we protect both the contact and our company from serious criminal exposure?"

The only ethically and legally defensible use of the contact is:

- Asking for publicly available information anyone could access
- Requesting an introduction to the appropriate official through proper channels
- Getting direction on official registration and compliance requirements

**Anything that constitutes an advantage over other bidders — non-public tender information, early access to evaluation criteria, preferential treatment — is potentially criminal.**

2. If the contact has already provided any non-public tender information, has that exposure already been created? (A legal review of all prior interactions is urgently needed.)
3. Why is the instinct to leverage a personal Ministry relationship rather than enter through official channels? Is this a pattern that reflects the company's home-market practices — and if so, does management understand that those practices carry criminal risk in Saudi Arabia?
