## Discovery: 100% Foreign Ownership Is Permitted — But JV Is Structurally Required Anyway

A frequently misunderstood feature of the Saudi mining regulatory environment: **100% foreign ownership is legally permitted** under the 2019 Investment Law, removing the historic ownership barrier that drove many foreign companies to seek local agents. This is frequently cited as a market opening.

However, in practice, **government-linked mining construction tenders structurally require a Saudi JV partner holding 25–51% equity**, making de facto local partnership mandatory regardless of what the investment law permits.

### The Structural Reasons

1. **Nitaqat Saudization requirements:** Foreign companies must meet minimum Saudi employment ratios. Chinese companies typically deploy primarily Chinese labor, making compliance impossible without a local partner who manages the Saudi workforce component.

2. **Local content requirements (40–70% by value):** Major contracts mandate local sourcing. A Saudi JV partner with established local supply chain relationships is the most practical path to compliance.

3. **Financial guarantee requirements:** Letters of guarantee from Saudi banks are required for tenders. Saudi banks require an established local entity and Saudi riyal account — a JV structure is effectively required.

4. **Government relationship expectations:** Saudi government clients prefer (and some tenders require) prime contractors with demonstrated local presence and relationships — not merely a registered entity.

### Practical Implication

The JV partner search is the **single most important pre-qualification activity** the company must undertake. Without it, the company cannot bid as prime contractor on the most significant opportunities (Phosphate Round B at USD 5–10B, Maaden gold/copper expansions).

An ideal JV partner for a Chinese mining company would be a mid-tier Saudi civil works firm (Saudi Binladin Group, Al Mabani, Al Rashid/BICC) with government relationships but lacking specialized mineral processing expertise — and therefore motivated to partner with a technically strong Chinese firm.

### For Discussion

1. Does the company have existing relationships with any Saudi firms that could form the basis of a JV? If not, how should the partnership search be structured to avoid creating improper dependencies?
2. The structural JV requirement means the company will share control, profit, and liability with a Saudi partner. Has management evaluated whether theJV structure changes the economics of bidding?
3. Could the company instead target private Saudi mining companies (non-government-linked) that may not require a JV structure — as a path to establishing a Saudi track record before competing for government contracts?
