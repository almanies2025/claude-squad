## Discovery: EPC Contract Realistic IRR Is 15–22% for Chinese Contractors

**The most important financial finding from the updated analysis.**

### The IRR Range

For a Chinese EPC contractor with Chinese labor, Chinese supply chains, and Chinese policy bank financing executing a Saudi mining construction contract on time:

- **Conservative (Saudi bank financing, 12-month delay):** 8–14% IRR
- **Base case (Chinese policy financing, on-time):** 15–20% IRR
- **Optimistic (policy financing + strong supply chain margins + Kingdom premium):** 22–28% IRR

**The realistic expected IRR is 15–22%.**

### Why the Range Is Wide — And What Drives Each Scenario

**The biggest variable is financing cost:**

- Chinese policy bank financing (EXIM Bank, CDB) at 2–4% vs Saudi local bank financing at 6–8% = 3–5 percentage point IRR difference
- Saudi Arabia is NOT a traditional BRI market — policy bank appetite for Saudi mining specifically is unconfirmed and requires an indicative term sheet

**The second biggest variable is delay:**

- Saudi government projects routinely delay 6–12 months
- LD exposure at 0.5–1%/week can eliminate an entire contract's profit margin
- Chinese labor and shift schedules are the primary tool to recover schedule

**The third variable is procurement supply chain markup:**

- Sourcing equipment through Chinese manufacturers at 20–40% below Western-equivalent cost, then marking up in the EPC contract, is a significant margin driver that most Western competitors cannot replicate

### Critical Data Points

1. **Maaden counterparty is strong:** $10.3B revenue, $2B net profit in 2025 (+156% YoY), EBITDA margins ~40%+. The company is in a massive CAPEX cycle ($7.55–9.55B CAPEX guidance 2025). This is not a distressed counterparty — contract payment risk is low.

2. **Saudi construction market contracted 60% in 2025:** Awards fell from $71B (2024) to under $30B (2025). This means more competition for fewer contracts — making the existing EPC contract position more strategically valuable, not less.

3. **Chinese firms already have $5B+ in Saudi EPC contracts** — the market is proven open to Chinese entrants. The opportunity is real.

### For Discussion

1. Does the company have an indicative EXIM Bank or CDB term sheet for Saudi mining projects? If yes, the base case (15–20%) applies. If no, the conservative case (8–14%) is the appropriate planning assumption until financing is confirmed.

2. Has a schedule risk analysis been done on the existing EPC contract? The delay LD exposure should be quantified before any IRR estimate is finalized.

3. Is the company sourcing equipment through Chinese manufacturers with markup embedded in the EPC contract — or is equipment being sourced at cost with no procurement margin? This alone could represent 10–15% of contract value.
